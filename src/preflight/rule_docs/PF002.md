# PF002  Clock / baud mismatch

## What goes wrong
A UART's baud generator divides the clock of the APB bus it sits on (PCLK1
or PCLK2) by a divisor N (USARTDIV = fck / (16 x baud), stored as mantissa
plus a 4-bit fraction). Hardware can only produce fck / N for a whole N, so
the real baud is close to, but rarely exactly, the one you asked for. The
gap grows when fck is small compared to the baud rate.

The receiver samples each bit near its middle. Over a 10-bit frame the
timing error of both ends must stay well below half a bit; a practical
budget is about 2% per end. Beyond that you get framing errors and garbage
characters, usually only at one baud rate or only on some boards.

Typical causes: an APB prescaler or PLL change made after the baud rate was
chosen, or checking the wrong bus (on F4, USART1/USART6 are on APB2, the
other USARTs on APB1).

## Example
STM32F4, HSI 16 MHz, APB1 prescaler /8 -> PCLK1 = 2 MHz, USART2 at 115200:

    N = round(2 000 000 / 115 200) = 17
    actual baud = 2 000 000 / 17 = 117 647   (+2.12%)   -> error

Same baud with PCLK1 = 42 MHz: N = 365, actual 115 068 (-0.11%) -> fine.

## How to fix
Raise the peripheral clock (lower the APB prescaler, use the PLL), lower the
baud rate, or switch to 8x oversampling for finer steps at high baud. A
crystal whose frequency divides well into the baud rate also helps.

## What Preflight checks
It derives PCLK1/PCLK2 from the PLL/prescaler settings (STM32F1 and F4),
computes the divisor exactly as the HAL does, and reports the real error:
above 1% = warning, above 2% = error, divisor out of range = "unreachable".
If the file also records a peripheral clock that disagrees with the derived
one, that is a warning (stale or hand-edited file). Other families use the
frequency CubeMX recorded, labelled as such.
